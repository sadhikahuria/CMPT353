1. Of log, exp, sqrt, and square, sqrt got closest to satisfying the T-test assumptions: one
   group still failed normality, the other passed, and equal-variance passed cleanly.

2. Original T-test: if we could reject the null, it would mean the average number of comments
   per day differs between weekdays and weekends -- but the assumptions are violated, so this
   conclusion isn't trustworthy.
   Transformed T-test: if we could reject the null, it would mean the square root of the daily
   comment count differs between weekdays and weekends -- valid on the transformed scale, but
   the violated weekday normality assumption still makes it shaky, and it's a less direct
   statement about the actual comment counts.

3. The weekly-aggregated T-test and the Mann-Whitney U-test are the two trustworthy approaches,
   sincefirt they're the only ones whose assumptions actually hold. Between those, I think the
   Mann-Whitney test does a better job answering the original question, since it works directly
   on the original daily counts without aggregating them into weekly averages (which throws away
   information and subtly changes the question to a weekly one).

4. More comments are posted on weekdays (average ~1823.6) than on weekends (average ~1269.5).
